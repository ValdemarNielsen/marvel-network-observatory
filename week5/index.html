03 · The vocabulary race

Who introduces the Marvel vocabulary first?

We added pages in two orders: most-linked characters first and least-linked characters first. The finishing vocabulary is identical. The route there is not.

Careful:

This is not proof that famous characters use more original language. Their articles are much longer, so famous-first exposes more tokens sooner. Page order and document size are tangled together.

04 · Close the file

Fame buys ink.
It does not set the price.

Incoming links and article length have Spearman correlation 0.752. The log-log regression explains 59% of page-length variation. That is a real relationship with plenty of room for publishing history, alternate identities, adaptations, and Wikipedia editing culture.

We can claim

Within this frozen 303-page corpus, characters linked by more peers usually have longer articles.

We cannot claim

Links cause longer pages, or word count measures cultural importance, popularity, or article quality.

The useful surprise

Brian Braddock and Quasar show why a regression should lead back to the documents, not replace reading them.

Three rules pinned over our desk

  1. 01Count before interpreting.
  2. 02Open the winning document.
  3. 03Name what the metric cannot see.

Preprocessing receipt

303 rendered Wikipedia articles. Word-like tokens matched with [A-Za-z]+, lowercased. Punctuation is not counted. Possessives and contractions stay together. The network measure is in-degree from the frozen Week 1 directed graph.

Model receipt

Ordinary least squares on log(words) versus log(1 + in-degree). “More” and “less than expected” refer only to this fitted relationship.
